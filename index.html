The decision in front of you

It usually starts with one decision that’s hard to undo.

Each one touches taxes, entities, coverage, lending and investments at the same time. Most families meet it while they’re still building, with the most to gain and the most to lose.

  1. 01

    Selling the business.

    What you net, what you owe, and what you live on after.

  2. 02

    Buying the land.

    Cash, debt, and what it ties up for years.

  3. 03

    A liquidity event.

    The money arrives at once. The decisions around it don’t.

  4. 04

    Helping the kids.

    Generous now, without shortchanging later.

Why separate advice leaves gaps

Five good advisors. Five separate drawings.

Each professional works from their own sheet, and each sheet is accurate. But a sale changes the tax picture, which changes the trust, which changes the coverage, which changes the loan. The blind spots live between the sheets, where no one is assigned to look.

CPA

Sees this year’s return.

Attorney

Advisor

Laid over one another, the five fragments become one mark. That is the planning view Continuum builds, and every advisor can work from it.

Signs your family needs one view

You may already be the only one holding the whole picture.

  1. i.

    Your advisors have never been in the same conversation.

    Each one hears your plans secondhand, if at all.

  2. ii.

    Good advice from one quietly contradicts another.

    A tax move that works against the estate plan. Coverage that no longer fits the balance sheet.

  3. iii.

    The decision has a date on it.

    A buyer, a closing, a deadline. Someone else set the timeline.

  4. iv.

    You can’t say what the move does to the next ten years.

    You know it’s significant. You can’t yet see how.

  5. v.

    Your plan was built for a smaller life.

    The documents still describe the family you were.

  6. vi.

    You’re the one translating between everyone.

    Usually in the evenings, from memory.

Who it’s for

Built for a particular family, at a particular moment.

Likely a good fit

  • Families still building wealth, with a decision ahead that’s hard to reverse.
  • People who already have good advisors and want them working from the same page.
  • Owners weighing a sale, a transition or a large purchase.
  • Parents who want to help their children without guessing at the cost.

Likely not the right fit

  • Anyone shopping for a product first. We start with the plan.
  • Someone looking for a quick opinion on a single investment.
  • Families who would rather not see the whole picture.
